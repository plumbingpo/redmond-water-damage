A sewage backup is one of the most hazardous situations a property owner can face. Anyone who comes in contact with contaminated material is exposed to parasites, bacteria and viruses, among them giardia, E. coli, salmonella and hepatitis, all carried in raw sewage and all dangerous. Redmond Restoration provides emergency sewage cleanup throughout Redmond, responding 24/7 with properly trained technicians, appropriate personal protective equipment and EPA-registered disinfectants to decontaminate your property safely.

The industry classifies sewage as black water, or Category 3, which is the most dangerous and most contaminated type of water damage there is. That rating calls for the strictest protocols: containment barriers go up, crews wear full protective equipment, every porous material the sewage touched is removed, and the surfaces that stay are disinfected thoroughly. We work to the IICRC S500 standard that governs Category 3 losses, with the goal of returning your property to a condition safe to occupy.

The cleanup process involves removing all standing sewage, extracting contaminated water from structural materials, removing and properly disposing of every porous material that contacted sewage (carpet, padding, drywall, insulation), thoroughly disinfecting hard surfaces, applying antimicrobial treatments and completing structural drying to prevent secondary mold growth. We also deodorize the affected area with industrial-grade hydroxyl generators and thermal fogging equipment to eliminate lingering sewage odors.

Why Professional Sewage Cleanup Matters

No homeowner should try to clean up sewage personally. The biological hazards in raw sewage require specialized training, proper personal protective equipment (Tyvek suits, respirators, protective gloves and boots) and EPA-registered disinfection products that are not available to consumers. Exposure to sewage pathogens can cause serious illness, including gastrointestinal disease, hepatitis, respiratory infections and skin infections.

Contaminated materials count as biohazard waste, and disposing of them properly is part of a professional cleanup. Putting carpet, insulation or drywall soaked with sewage out with household trash breaks the law and endangers sanitation workers and your community. Licensed biohazard disposal facilities take this waste from certified restoration firms, which keep the required protocols in place.

We dry out any moisture that remains using commercial equipment. Sewage odors are eliminated through industrial deodorizing. Finally, a clearance test verifies that people can safely use the area again.

Maintenance Tips for Sewage Cleanup

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Have roots removed where trees grow by the sewer, before they get inside the pipe and plug it
  • Know the location and condition of your sewer cleanout for emergency access
  • Use drain strainers in showers and sinks to catch hair and debris
  • Every 2 to 3 years, get a sewer camera run through the line to spot developing issues
  • Keep cooking oil, grease and food scraps out of the drain, where they build into clogs
  • Flush nothing but toilet paper, because wipes, even 'flushable' ones, cause clogs

How to Tell You Need Sewage Cleanup

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Lower levels or drain openings giving off a heavy sewage odor, sometimes like rotten eggs
  • A cleanout that weeps sewage, or wet foul patches in the lawn along the sewer path
  • Toilets or drains that gurgle whenever you run water somewhere else
  • Foul-smelling moisture showing up on flooring or along the walls
  • Every sink, tub and shower draining slowly even after you plunge them
  • A toilet overflow containing waste that has spread onto flooring or carpet
  • People in the household getting stomach or intestinal symptoms with no clear cause
